## 3. About patient testimonials

You asked me to check whether testimonials could be used with faces blurred and personal
details removed. **I checked, and my answer is that we should not put them on the website.
Blurring does not solve the problem.**

In plain terms: Indian medical advertising rules restrict practitioners from using patient
testimonials, patient stories and before-and-after material to promote a practice, and the
guidance states that a patient's consent does not by itself make it permissible. Blurring is
treated as an extra safeguard for material that is already allowed, not as a way to make
restricted material allowed. Separately, there is a law covering advertisements claiming to
treat or cure certain listed conditions which applies to whoever publishes the advertisement,
with real penalties attached.

Since the website and hosting will be in your name, you are the publisher. So this is your
decision with your own legal advisor — but I will not build it, because the downside falls on
your registration and I do not think it is worth the risk.

**What goes in that section instead:**

- **A link to your Google reviews.** Patients write those on Google of their own accord. It is
  the same social proof, on someone else's platform, and you are not reproducing it.
- **Your practitioners on camera** explaining treatments. Your team's authority is your real
  strength and nothing restricts it.
- **Hard credentials** — years of practice, qualifications, in-house medicine preparation, and
  photographs of the real clinic.

One more thing: treatment pages will describe **what a treatment involves and what it helps
with**, not that it cures a named disease. Same reason.

## 7a. Your four domains — pick one as the main address

You own `daiviksatva.com`, `daiviksatva.in`, `daiviksatva.org` and `daiviksatva.co.in`. Holding
all four is sensible — it stops anyone else taking your name.

**But the website should live on only one of them.** If the same site answers on all four,
Google sees four copies of it and has to guess which is the real one, which weakens all of them.
The other three should simply forward to the main one. That way you keep the protection and the
website keeps its full strength.

**My recommendation: `daiviksatva.com` as the main address**, with the other three forwarding to
it. It is the one people type without thinking, and it is what works best on a signboard, a
visiting card or a WhatsApp forward. `daiviksatva.in` would also be a perfectly good choice —
the important thing is choosing one, not which one. Tell me your preference and I will set the
rest up to forward.

Two practical notes:

- **Turn on automatic renewal for all four.** `daiviksatva.com` expires first, on 5 July 2027.
  A domain that lapses takes the website down with it, and the name can be bought by someone
  else.
- Your email, if it runs on one of these domains, keeps working either way — forwarding the
  website does not affect email.
