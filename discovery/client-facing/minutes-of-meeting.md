## 7. One important point on patient testimonials

You said patient testimonials are one of your strongest assets, and asked whether they could be
used with faces blurred and personal details removed. I looked into this properly rather than
answer on the spot.

**My finding is that I should not put patient testimonials on the website, and blurring faces
does not solve the problem.** Indian medical advertising rules restrict practitioners from
using patient testimonials, stories and before-and-after material for promotion, and the
guidance is explicit that a patient's consent does not by itself make it permissible. Blurring
is treated as an additional safeguard for material that is already allowed — not as a way to
make restricted material allowed. There is a separate law covering advertisements that claim to
treat or cure specific listed conditions, which carries real penalties and applies to anyone
publishing such an advertisement, not only to doctors.

I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice. The website and hosting will be in your name,
which makes you the publisher, so this is ultimately your call with your own legal advisor. But
I am not willing to build something that puts your registration at risk, and I would be doing
you a disservice if I did.

**What I propose instead**, which I think is genuinely as persuasive:

- A link to your **Google reviews**, where patients post of their own accord on Google's
  platform rather than on your site.
- **Your practitioners on camera**, explaining what a treatment involves and what to expect.
  Your team's authority is your strongest asset and it carries no restriction at all.
- **The clinic's own credentials** — years of practice, qualifications, your in-house medicine
  preparation, and photographs of the actual clinic.

This is covered in more detail in the website plan. I am happy to talk it through.
